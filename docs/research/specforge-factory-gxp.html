Design review · SpecForge Software Factory · GxP · 9 Oct 2026

It can make compliance continuous. It cannot make software compliant on its own.

This review judges the factory as designed, assuming every piece gets built. As a design it is strong. It turns validation from documents written after the fact into evidence the build produces on every change. Whether regulated companies and inspectors accept it depends on six blocking points, and none of them is a coding task.

What it changesValidation becomes a property of every commit.

Trace matrix, risk links, executed evidence, change impact and the validation report are generated and re-checked continuously, not assembled before an audit.

What it cannot doProve that content is true or adequate.

Rules prove that links exist and records are complete. Whether a risk assessment is right, or a test is meaningful, stays a human judgement, and the regulated company stays accountable.

What blocks itTrust, not code.

Validating the rules themselves, structure being mistaken for truth, trusted identity and time, AI changing under you, regulator acceptance, and data integrity at the boundaries.

1 · Scorecard

Where the design is strong and where it is not

Ten GxP capabilities, each scored 0 to 5 for the factory as designed, against today's typical practice of validated documents plus an ALM or eQMS. The factory wins clearly on traceability, change impact and AI governance. It is roughly even on signatures. It is weaker on long-term records, where paper-equivalent PDFs in a validated document system are hard to beat.

2 · How it ensures compliance

The GAMP 5 V-model, built by the factory

GAMP 5 describes validation as a V. Specifications go down the left side, each verified by a matching test level on the right. Risk decides how much rigour each step needs, and change control wraps around everything. The factory maps each part of the V to a hard skill and keeps the horizontal links checked on every commit. The validation report at the top is generated from the graph, never written by hand.

ALCOA+: how each data-integrity principle is met

3 · The central risk

Structure is not truth

The factory gives confidence in layers, and each layer proves less than it appears to. The danger is automation bias: a green check gets read as "compliant" when it only means "linked". SpecForge's own repository already showed this. In its September 2026 audit, 584 of 1,562 linked tests (37%) were weak, mismatched or checked nothing, although every link was valid.

4 · Who is accountable

The factory supports compliance; the regulated company satisfies it

Bridger's spec put it well: a library supports a regulation, and only the regulated system satisfies it. The same holds for the factory. GAMP 5 leaves categorisation and risk acceptance to the regulated user, and Part 11 places signature certification on the company. The factory provider can make all of this easy. It cannot own it.

5 · Blocking points

Eleven blockers, six of them critical

These are design, regulatory and organisational blockers. None of them is about code maturity, and none goes away by writing more code. Each is placed by how hard it is to resolve and how much it affects acceptance by QA and inspectors. Start in the top-right corner.

Proposed GxP release gate

A release can be signed only when all of these hold

  • trace completeness on GxP-critical requirements = 100%
  • executed-evidence ratio = 100% (no hand-written evidence)
  • proof strength on GxP-critical requirements ≥ 80% mutation score
  • signature validity = 100% and trail integrity = 100%
  • independent review on high-risk changes = 100%
  • no expired waivers; every active waiver signed by QA
  • no open suspect items older than the release candidate

What a pilot should prove

Hypothesis 1weeks → hours

Time to an inspection-ready evidence pack for a release drops from weeks of document work to hours, because the pack is generated.

Hypothesis 280 / 20 flips

CSA discussions often describe validation effort as mostly documentation and a little testing. The factory should flip that ratio toward testing and critical thinking.

Hypothesis 30 late finds

Traceability and change-control gaps are found at the edit that causes them, never at QA review or inspection.

Guardrail≥ 80%

Rule precision stays high enough that people keep reading the flags. If it falls, the rules are tuned before scope grows.

Verdict

The right design for GxP. The work that remains is earning trust.

The factory's core idea is sound: put the standards in hard skills, let agents draft, let the compiler prove structure on every change, and route only judgement to people. That matches where GAMP 5's second edition and FDA's Computer Software Assurance are heading, with risk-based rigour, critical thinking and less paperwork. What decides success is whether a QA head and an inspector will rely on it. That needs validated rules, trusted signatures, honest labels and a pilot that passes a mock inspection. Solve B1, B2, B3 and B5 first. Everything else follows.
